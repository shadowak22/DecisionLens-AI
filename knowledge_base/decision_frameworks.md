# Comprehensive Decision-Making Frameworks

## 1. Multi-Criteria Decision Analysis (MCDA)
Multi-Criteria Decision Analysis provides a systematic methodology for evaluating multiple conflicting criteria in complex decisions. Key components include:
- **Criteria Identification**: Isolating non-overlapping quantitative and qualitative dimensions (e.g., cost, user experience, compliance, technical debt).
- **Weight Allocation**: Normalizing stakeholder weights through techniques like Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) or pairwise comparison.
- **Scoring Matrix**: Rating alternatives against criteria on standardized ordinal scales (1-10) to compute aggregate weighted utility scores.

## 2. The Cynefin Framework for Decision Contexts
Developed by Dave Snowden, Cynefin categorizes problems into five distinct operational domains:
- **Clear (Simple)**: Best practice applies; sense-categorize-respond. Standard operating procedures suffice.
- **Complicated**: Good practice applies; sense-analyze-respond. Requires expert investigation and trade-off analysis.
- **Complex**: Emergent practice applies; probe-sense-respond. Unpredictable outcomes require safe-to-fail experimentation and iterative feedback loops.
- **Chaotic**: Novel practice applies; act-sense-respond. Immediate stabilization is needed before systemic analysis.
- **Confused (Aporia)**: Domain undetermined; requires decomposition into constituent sub-problems.

## 3. Six Thinking Hats (Edward de Bono)
Structured perspective division prevents cognitive bias and groupthink:
- **White Hat**: Neutral facts, empirical data, and information gaps.
- **Red Hat**: Intuition, emotional reactions, and gut stakeholder sentiment.
- **Black Hat**: Caution, risk identification, critical flaw exposure, and worst-case scenarios.
- **Yellow Hat**: Optimism, upside potential, strategic benefits, and feasibility.
- **Green Hat**: Creativity, out-of-the-box alternatives, and innovative workarounds.
- **Blue Hat**: Metacognition, process governance, synthesis, and decision orchestration.

## 4. Reversible vs. Irreversible Decisions (Type 1 vs. Type 2)
Jeff Bezos' classification framework for organizational velocity:
- **Type 1 (One-Way Doors)**: High consequence, irreversible or near-irreversible. Requires exhaustive analysis, multi-stakeholder consensus, and deep deliberation.
- **Type 2 (Two-Way Doors)**: Easily reversible with modest cost. Should be made swiftly by individuals or small teams using lightweight experimentation.
